## Context

For executive leadership at Shubham EPC, getting an accurate picture of ongoing projects, cash flow requirements, billing status, and material consumption required waiting for weekly or monthly review meetings. 

By the time leadership received department spreadsheets, the numbers were already days old, compiled under differing assumptions, and frequently challenged during the meeting itself.

## The Problem

- **Disputed Definitions:** Accounts calculated billing based on issued invoices; project managers calculated progress based on site execution; procurement calculated costs based on purchase orders. No two departments agreed on actual project margins.
- **Manual Spreadsheet Compilations:** Senior department managers spent up to `{{METRIC: manager hours spent per week compiling reports}}` every Monday consolidating multiple Excel files.
- **Lagging Indicator Management:** Leadership learned about budget overruns and delayed milestones only after invoices piled up, rather than having early warning alerts.

## What I Did

The technical effort of making charts is secondary; the real work was standardising business logic and automating the pipeline:

1. **Metric Definition Alignment:** Facilitated alignment sessions between project directors, accounts, and procurement to define single, authoritative formulas for billing, work-in-progress (WIP), and procurement burn rates.
2. **Automated ETL Pipeline:** Built automated data extraction flows that pull records directly from operational databases and registers every night, transforming raw numbers into structured data models.
3. **Executive MIS Dashboard:** Developed clean, role-tailored dashboards providing directors with headline project KPIs, drill-down capabilities by site, and automated variance flags.
4. **Automated Exception Triggers:** Configured alert notifications sent to leadership when project expenditure exceeds budget thresholds by more than `{{METRIC: percentage threshold}}`%.

## Outcome

- Eliminated `{{METRIC: total hours saved in reporting prep}}` per month previously spent manually compiling Excel reports.
- Real-time decision making: Executives now review live project data with zero lag instead of waiting for monthly wrap-up presentations.
- Ended metric disputes in executive reviews because all departments operate from a single, auditable source of truth.
